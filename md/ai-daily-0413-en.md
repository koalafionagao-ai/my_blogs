### 1. *[ORNN compute price index: Renting one NVIDIA Blackwell GPU now costs $4.08/hour, up 48%](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/ai-is-using-so-much-energy-that-computing-firepower-is-running-out-156e5c85)*

### 2. *[A profile of the Biological Computing Company, which uses living neurons to build AI chips](https://www.thedeepview.com/articles/ai-s-next-big-chip-bet-may-be-biological)*

> [!Note]
> [[AI’s next big chip bet may be biological]]> 
> The core judgment of this article—that "**biological chips will become AI's next major bet**"—carries extremely high credibility. This is no longer a sci-fi concept, but a hardware paradigm shift that is genuinely accelerating in 2026 (commonly referred to in the industry as Bio-computing or Organoid Intelligence).
> 
> When handling massive LLM inference, relying solely on the expansion of silicon chips is facing extremely high physical and power cost barriers. Introducing "Wetware" technology, which combines living nerve cells with silicon interfaces, is precisely aimed at breaking this computing power bottleneck. In fact, the trend explored in this article aligns perfectly with recent industry movements: just in March 2026, the fast-growing data center operator DayOne announced plans to build "Bio Data Centers" in Singapore and Melbourne, adopting on a large scale biological chips like the CL1 developed by Cortical Labs (which deploys cultured neurons on silicon chips). Around the same time, researchers at the University of California, Santa Cruz (UCSC) successfully trained brain organoids to solve the classic engineering "cart-pole problem."
> 
> Around bio-computing, the scientific and engineering communities are currently in a state of extreme polarization:
> 
> - **The Proponents (Focus on Ultimate Energy Efficiency):** In system design considerations under global constraints like energy depletion, biological chips are seen as the key to breaking the deadlock. The human brain processes complex information with extremely low power consumption (requiring only a few to a dozen watts). Supporters believe that the natural parallel processing capability and plasticity of biological organs can complete currently hyper-power-consuming AI inference tasks with minimal energy consumption. Furthermore, introducing this into computing architectures is even being packaged by many enterprises as an ESG-compliant (Environmental, Social, and Governance) "sustainable computing" strategy, attracting massive capital.
>     
> - **Radical Controversy (Ethics and "Brain Farming"):** The most radical opposing voices in the industry focus on the ethical shock of what is dubbed "Brain Farming." Putting cultured human (or animal) nerve cells into server racks to act as the "underlying computing units" for LLMs or complex agents is currently in a total regulatory vacuum. Opponents strongly question the conscious boundaries of such systems, as well as the moral baseline of completely commercializing biological tissue for cloud computing power rental.
>     
> - **Feedback at the Engineering Architecture Level (Black Box and Uncertainty):** From the perspective of technical implementation, feedback from senior developers and architects is "huge potential but extremely uncontrollable." Biological tissues have a limited lifespan, and their reproducibility when interacting with electronic interfaces is poor. More importantly, compared to traditional silicon systems with highly deterministic ledger logic and mature gradient descent algorithms, cell-level learning mechanisms are not yet fully understood. For automated workflows that demand high concurrency and high determinism, current biological chips remain an absolute black box that is difficult to debug.
>     
> 
> Overall, bio-AI chips are a disruptive route forced out by real physical (energy) pain points. It has already moved from labs to early commercial data center pilots, making it one of the most controversial yet high-potential "scale judgment" nodes in the current evolution of underlying computing power.
